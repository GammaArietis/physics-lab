# W7 — Scalar-completed gauge–Yukawa and electromagnetic RG campaign

W7 closes the scalar-quartic sector of the W6 model at the mixed order used in
the original perturbative analysis: gauge two-loop, Yukawa one-loop, scalar
quartics one-loop and Abelian QED one-loop. The added scalar equations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_h &= -(11+2\epsilon)\alpha_y^2
 +4\alpha_h(\alpha_y+2\alpha_h),\\
\beta_v &= 12\alpha_h^2
 +4\alpha_v(\alpha_v+4\alpha_h+\alpha_y).
\end{aligned}
\]

For every scanned positive \(\epsilon\), both scalar roots are retained. The
branch usually denoted FP1 has the stable quartic-potential proxy; FP2 has the
opposite sign. The laboratory records both and performs no automatic selection.

## Campaign

- 8 values of \(\epsilon\);
- both scalar fixed-point branches;
- 3 non-zero electromagnetic seeds per branch;
- 48 coupled trajectories;
- residuals and 5-coupling stability matrices recorded;
- no experimental data and no alpha fitting.

## Result

The gauge–Yukawa–scalar fixed-point structure survives this closure of the
scalar sector. The electromagnetic coordinate still remains

\[
\alpha_{\rm em}^*=0.
\]

Thus the result is stronger than W6 in one sense: adding the scalar quartics
does not remove the interacting non-Abelian fixed-point mechanism. It is also
equally clear that this mechanism still does not determine the non-zero
electromagnetic coupling and therefore does not derive \(1/137\).

The larger \(\epsilon\) values are stress diagnostics, not automatically
perturbative predictions. Higher-loop calculations of the Litim–Sannino model
exist and are recorded in the bibliography; comparison with them is the next
consistency gate.
